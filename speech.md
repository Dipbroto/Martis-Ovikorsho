## Technical Defense & Judge Q&A Cheat Sheet

### 1. "Where does your elevation and terrain data come from?"
> *"We ingested the official USGS Astrogeology and ESA HRSC-MOLA blended digital elevation model at 200 meters per pixel across our hero landing sites (Jezero, Gale, Gusev, Meridiani, Mawrth, Valles Marineris, and Olympus Mons), complemented by the global MGS MOLA 4 pixel-per-degree grid. Slope gradients and radio line-of-sight are computed directly on these elevation meshes via Web Workers."*

### 2. "How do you run 38 ML engines inside the browser without latency?"
> *"Instead of shipping heavy 500 MB PyTorch or ONNX runtimes that freeze client browsers, we trained all models offline in Python against NASA PDS archives. We then exported normalized regression weights, intercept biases, standard scalers, and 90% confidence intervals into a single 44.7 KB JSON bundle. At runtime, inference is executed via native, vectorized JavaScript dot-products in under 0.5 milliseconds."*

### 3. "What makes your routing different from standard Google Maps or A*?"
> *"Standard pathfinders minimize geometric Euclidean distance. MarsWalk minimizes **dynamic energy and risk** using Bekker-Wong soil mechanics. The edge cost function is $C = E_{\text{Wh}} \times (1 + 4(s/100)^2)$. When slope and low thermal inertia indicate loose drift sand with slip $>45\%$ or entrapment probability $>65\%$, the cost approaches infinity, forcing the router to find firm regolith or bedrock pavements."*

### 4. "Is your weather and radiation model truly calibrated to Mars?"
> *"Yes. Our atmospheric model was trained on 4,745 consecutive sols of Curiosity REMS telemetry from Gale Crater, with 79 hardware ADC dropouts cleansed via smooth local linear interpolation ($T_{\max}\text{ }R^2 = 0.8884, P\text{ }R^2 = 0.9977$). The radiation model is calibrated to Curiosity MSL RAD surface dosimetry (Hassler et al. Science 2014) modulated by the 11-year Schwabe solar cycle."*

### 5. "How does the Science Lab handle Raman fluorescence?"
> *"Planetary Raman spectroscopy suffers from intense mineral fluorescence. Our SHERLOC engine applies asymmetric least squares baseline subtraction followed by 16-dimensional latent manifold deconvolution, separating the aromatic carbon D and G bands (1350 and 1580 cm⁻¹) from noise and matching against our astrobiology organic reference library."*
